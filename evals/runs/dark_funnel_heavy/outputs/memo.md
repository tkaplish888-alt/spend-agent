## Last week's moves
First run, so there is nothing to review.

## What's happening
- **Direct:** conversions rose from 33/week (Aug 3) to 152/week (Oct 26), and revenue from $6.2k to $28.8k. Paid spend was flat. Self-reported "Asked ChatGPT" plus "AI assistant" answers rose from about 16% to 41% of responses. Something untracked is driving buyers, and no paid channel should claim it.
- **Google Search:** conversions rose from about 240/week to about 400/week on flat spend of about $4.2k. CPA fell from $17.6 to $10.4, and reported ROAS is 18.2. This looks like branded search catching AI-driven demand, so I am not scaling it.
- **Meta:** CPA is $62 over the last 3 weeks vs $60.5 earlier. ROAS is about 3 and above the 1.5 target under every model. Self-reported Instagram/Facebook share fell from 20% to 9.5%, which is worth watching.
- **LinkedIn:** CPA is $110 vs $140 earlier. Last-touch ROAS is 1.42, below target, but every other model gives 1.9–2.5, so I'm leaving it.
- **Email:** last week's CPA of $29.7 comes from a weak week with 8 conversions on 7 days of spend (2 zero-conversion days). The 3-week CPA is flat at $19.6 vs $19.1, so I'm treating it as noise.
- No channel has CPA up 40% or more, and no channel had a zero-conversion week.

## Moves
None. Google $600/day, Meta $500/day, LinkedIn $300/day and Email $40/day are unchanged. Cutting is not justified, and scaling on credit that may belong to AI-driven demand is not safe.

## Where the models disagree
- **Google:** credit share runs from 43% (first-touch) to 65% (last-touch), and implied ROAS from 9.4 to 14.3. Every model is above target, but the true value is uncertain.
- **Email:** credit share runs from 3% (last-touch) to 16.5% (first-touch), and implied ROAS from 9.8 to 53.9. Email is undervalued by last-click.
- **Meta:** ROAS runs from 3.1 to 4.7.
- **LinkedIn:** ROAS runs from 1.4 to 2.5.
- Direct credit is fixed at 17% across models because it has no tracked touches.

## Test to run
Google brand-term geo holdout. Pause brand campaigns in about 20% of matched regions for 4 weeks. Compare total conversions across Google, direct and email, and the survey mix of "Google" vs AI answers.

## Confidence
Medium. The direct and survey trends are consistent, but both are correlational. Two results would change my mind:
- If the holdout shows brand search is truly incremental, I would scale Google.
- If direct falls back while spend stays flat, I would treat the AI effect as temporary.
